# The Election Commission of India

## Constitutional status

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is a constitutional body established under Article 324 of the Constitution. It is responsible for administering elections to the Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, state Legislative Assemblies, and the offices of President and Vice-President. State and local body elections are conducted by separate State Election Commissions.

## Composition

The ECI is a three-member body led by the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and two Election Commissioners. Their decisions are taken by majority vote. They are appointed by the President for a tenure of six years or until age 65, whichever comes first. The CEC can be removed only through impeachment by Parliament — the same process as a Supreme Court judge — making the office strongly insulated from political pressure.

## Key functions

The ECI's responsibilities span the full election cycle:

1. **Preparation of electoral rolls** — supervising the addition, deletion, and revision of voters
2. **Conduct of elections** — scheduling, deploying staff, providing EVMs, supervising polling and counting
3. **Recognition of political parties** — granting national and state-level recognition based on vote share and seat thresholds
4. **Allotment of symbols** — both reserved (for recognised parties) and free (for independents)
5. **Enforcement of the Model Code of Conduct** during elections
6. **Settling disputes** related to the recognition of parties and election symbols
7. **Advising on disqualification** of MPs and MLAs

## Tools used by the ECI

- **cVIGIL** — a citizen app for reporting MCC violations during elections, with 100-minute response guarantee
- **Suvidha portal** — for candidates to file nominations and get permissions for rallies
- **ENCORE** — for managing candidate affidavits, expenditures, and counting
- **Saksham** — accessibility-focused app for persons with disabilities

## Limits of ECI authority

The ECI can take action against violations of election law and the MCC, but it does not directly prosecute. Criminal complaints are filed with the police; the ECI can recommend FIRs and disqualify candidates.
