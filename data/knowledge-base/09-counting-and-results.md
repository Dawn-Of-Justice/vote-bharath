# Counting and Declaration of Results

## Counting day setup

Counting takes place at designated counting centres, usually one per constituency. Strong rooms holding the EVMs are opened in the presence of the Returning Officer, candidates or their counting agents, and ECI observers. The seals on each EVM are checked and verified before any counting begins.

## Order of counting

1. **Postal ballots** are counted first, starting at 8:00 AM. They are sorted candidate-wise and totalled.
2. **EVM counting** begins after the postal ballot count starts. Counting is done in rounds — typically 14 EVMs per round per table, with multiple tables running in parallel.
3. **VVPAT verification** comes at the end. The slips from 5 randomly selected booths per assembly segment are physically counted and matched against the EVM totals.

After each round, the Returning Officer announces the round-wise tally. These announcements are what news channels rely on for live result tracking.

## Declaration of result

When a candidate's lead becomes mathematically unassailable — meaning even if all remaining EVM votes went to the runner-up, the leader would still win — the Returning Officer formally declares the result and issues Form 21C (Certificate of Election) to the winner.

The full result, including round-wise breakdown, votes received by each candidate, postal ballot count, and NOTA tally, is published on the ECI Results Portal at results.eci.gov.in within hours of declaration.

## Recounting

Candidates can request a recount under Rule 63 of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961. The Returning Officer can grant or deny the request based on whether there is reasonable cause. Denied requests can be challenged in an Election Petition filed in the High Court within 45 days of the result.

## Form 17C

After polling closes at every booth, the Presiding Officer fills Form 17C — a record of total votes polled at that booth. A copy is given to each polling agent present. On counting day, Form 17C is the source-of-truth document used to reconcile EVM counts. The 17C records are public documents and are sometimes used by parties and journalists to audit the counting process.

## When are results declared

Lok Sabha and major state assembly results are typically declared on a single counting day, usually 3 to 5 days after the final phase of polling. Most close-contest seats settle by late evening; particularly tight races may be called only after midnight when the last round is counted.
