# NOTA — None Of The Above

## Origin

NOTA was introduced in Indian elections in 2013 following a Supreme Court judgement in PUCL vs Union of India. The court held that the right to reject all candidates is part of the freedom of expression guaranteed under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.

## How it appears on the EVM

NOTA is always the last button on the EVM, below all the candidates. Its symbol — a ballot paper with a black cross — was designed by the National Institute of Design, Ahmedabad. The button works exactly like any other vote: pressing it records a NOTA vote, the VVPAT prints a slip, and the choice is counted.

## What NOTA does — and does not do

A NOTA vote is a formal expression of dissatisfaction. It is counted and reported separately in the result. However, it currently has no electoral consequence: even if NOTA receives more votes than every candidate, the candidate with the highest non-NOTA vote count still wins.

Some State Election Commissions (Maharashtra, Haryana, Delhi, Puducherry) have ruled that if NOTA wins in local body elections, a fresh election must be held. The same rule has not been adopted at the Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha level.

## Why use NOTA

NOTA gives voters a way to:

- Register dissent without abstaining (your turnout is recorded)
- Signal to parties that they need to field better candidates
- Maintain the secrecy of the ballot — unlike Section 49O which required telling the Presiding Officer that you wished to abstain

In the 2019 Lok Sabha election, NOTA received about 1.04% of total votes — small in percentage terms but over 65 lakh individual votes.
