# Voter Rights and Protections

## Right to vote

The right to vote in India is a statutory right under Section 62 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, available to every adult citizen whose name appears on the electoral roll. While not a fundamental right per se, the right to a free and fair election has been held by the Supreme Court to be a basic feature of the Constitution.

## Right to information about candidates

Following the Supreme Court's judgement in Union of India vs Association for Democratic Reforms (2002), every candidate must file an affidavit (Form 26) disclosing:

- Criminal cases pending or convicted (with sentence details)
- Total assets — movable, immovable, and liabilities
- Educational qualifications
- Sources of income for the past five years
- Public dues to government bodies

These affidavits are public documents and are uploaded to the ECI website. Voters can look up any candidate before casting their vote.

## Right to a paid holiday on polling day

Section 135B of the Representation of the People Act mandates that every employer — including private companies — must give their employees a paid holiday on polling day in their constituency. Daily-wage workers are entitled to wages for the day. Violation can attract a fine of up to ₹500.

## Right to challenge a vote

If a polling official believes someone is impersonating a registered voter, the official can mark the vote as a "tendered vote" and the person can vote on a paper ballot, kept separately. If the original voter later turns up and proves their identity, the tendered ballot is not counted.

## Right to vote in secret

Voting in India is by secret ballot. The polling compartment is positioned so that no one — including officials and party agents — can see how a voter casts their vote. The VVPAT slip is visible only to the voter, then automatically dropped into a sealed box.

## Right to register a complaint

Voters can report violations through:

- **cVIGIL app** for Model Code violations during elections
- **Voter Helpline** (toll-free 1950) for any election-related grievance
- **ECI website complaint portal** for written complaints

All complaints are tracked with a unique number and the citizen receives status updates by SMS.

## Protections against intimidation

Voter intimidation, booth capturing, and offering bribes for votes are punishable offences under the Indian Penal Code and the Representation of the People Act. Sentences can range from fines to imprisonment up to 5 years. Booth capturing additionally voids the entire poll at that booth and triggers re-polling.
