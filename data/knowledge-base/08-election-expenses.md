# Election Expenses and Campaign Finance

## Spending limits for candidates

The Election Commission caps how much each candidate can spend on campaigning. Limits were last revised in January 2022:

- **Lok Sabha (larger states / UTs)**: ₹95 lakh per constituency
- **Lok Sabha (smaller states / UTs)**: ₹75 lakh per constituency
- **Vidhan Sabha (larger states)**: ₹40 lakh per constituency
- **Vidhan Sabha (smaller states)**: ₹28 lakh per constituency

These limits cover all expenses incurred between the date of nomination and the date of poll — posters, vehicles, public meetings, advertisements, and so on. Expenses incurred before nomination are not counted, which is a known loophole.

## What candidates must report

Within 30 days of the result being declared, each candidate must file a true account of election expenses with the District Election Officer. The account must be supported by vouchers, bills, and bank statements. Failure to file, or filing a false account, can lead to disqualification for up to three years under Section 10A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

## Limits on parties

There is no overall cap on what a political party can spend on a general election. However, party expenditure on individual candidates is loosely regulated, and parties must file annual contribution reports with the ECI disclosing donations above ₹20,000.

## Electoral bonds

Electoral bonds, introduced in 2018, allowed anonymous donations to political parties through interest-free instruments bought from the State Bank of India. The Supreme Court struck down the scheme in February 2024 as unconstitutional, ruling that it violated voters' right to information. The court ordered the SBI to disclose all bond purchases and redemptions, which were subsequently published by the ECI.

## How the ECI monitors

During elections, the ECI deploys Expenditure Observers, Flying Squads, Static Surveillance Teams, and Video Surveillance Teams in every constituency. Cash above ₹50,000 carried by anyone connected to a candidate can be seized for verification. Bank accounts opened by candidates for election expenses are monitored daily.
