# Postal Ballot and Special Voters

## Who can vote by post

Postal ballots are an exception to the in-person voting rule. They are available to:

1. **Service voters** — armed forces, paramilitary, and government employees posted outside their constituency
2. **Election duty voters** — polling staff, security forces, and ECI observers on duty during the election
3. **Voters under preventive detention**
4. **Senior citizens above 85 years**
5. **Persons with benchmark disabilities (40% or more)** with a disability certificate
6. **COVID-19 positive or suspect voters** during the pandemic period (rules extended periodically)

## How postal voting works for senior citizens and PwDs

Voters in categories 4 and 5 must opt in by submitting Form 12D to the Returning Officer at least 5 days before the date of notification. After verification, a polling team visits the voter's home with the ballot, a video camera for recording, and party agents (if they choose to attend). The voter marks the ballot in private, seals it, and the team carries it back to the Returning Officer.

## Counting

Postal ballots are counted first on counting day, before EVM counting begins. The candidate's marked preference must be clearly identifiable; ambiguous or improperly sealed ballots are rejected.

## NRI voting

NRIs (Non-Resident Indians) holding Indian passports can register as overseas electors using Form 6A. As of 2024, NRIs must still travel to their home constituency and vote in person — the proposal to allow NRI postal voting has been pending for years and is not yet operational.

## Migrant workers

Internal migrant workers cannot currently vote remotely. They must either travel home for polling day or transfer their voter registration to their current constituency. Several states are piloting "Remote Voting Machines" (RVMs) — modified EVMs that could in principle handle multi-constituency voting — but no general election has used them yet.
