# Model Code of Conduct (MCC)

## What it is

The Model Code of Conduct is a set of guidelines issued by the Election Commission of India that governs the conduct of political parties, candidates, and the government during elections. It comes into effect the moment the ECI announces the election schedule and remains in force until the results are declared.

The MCC is not a statutory law — it derives its authority from the consensus of political parties and the ECI's constitutional powers under Article 324. Most violations are dealt with by the ECI through warnings, censures, and prohibitions on campaigning. Serious violations that overlap with criminal law (like bribery or hate speech) are referred to police.

## Key restrictions on parties and candidates

- No appeal to caste or communal feelings to secure votes
- No criticism of opponents based on private life or unverified allegations
- No use of religious places for campaigning
- No bribery, intimidation, or impersonation of voters
- No serving liquor or distributing money on or near polling day
- No campaign activities within 200 metres of polling booths

## Key restrictions on the ruling party

This is what makes the MCC distinctive. Once it kicks in:

- Ministers cannot combine official tours with election work
- No new schemes, projects, or financial grants can be announced
- No appointments to public offices that could influence voters
- No use of government vehicles, machinery, or personnel for campaigning
- Government advertising featuring political leaders must stop

## Silence period

In the 48 hours before polling ends in any phase, all public campaigning, processions, and public meetings are banned. Door-to-door campaigning by candidates is allowed but loud-speakers, advertisements, and large gatherings are not. Exit polls cannot be published until polling concludes in all phases of the entire election.

## Reporting violations

Citizens can report MCC violations through the cVIGIL app, which allows uploading photos or 2-minute videos. Each complaint is geo-tagged and time-stamped, and the ECI commits to acting within 100 minutes — typically by sending a Flying Squad to investigate.
